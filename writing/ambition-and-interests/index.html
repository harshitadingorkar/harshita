On ambition & interests

An ongoing topic I visit often is my uneasiness with “ambition”, and a recent revelation has helped me figure out how to frame this. The revelation is that I must trust that my interest tells me where to put my effort. If I follow several interests at once, each iteration brings me closer to my truest self.

I notice this most on discovery days. At least once a week I set time aside to fall down rabbit holes: Are.na, odd websites, YouTube etc, really whatever tugs at me. Each time I’m down a rabbit hole, I feel connected, as if I’m doing what I’m supposed to be doing. It feels productive in a meaningful way, not a job way, but in the direction I’m meant to be heading.

That changes how I think about ambition. I used to measure it in promotions and money, and I struggled with it. A better definition: ambition is whatever gets me to my truest self. It isn’t linear. It’s a slow burn, an emerging discovery, and it can’t be read off a salary. I still care about those things, and I’d love to earn more. But they aren’t the whole goal. The goal is to explore my interests and find out what I can contribute to making the world better.

So I’m going to collect and pursue every passion. Anything I’m curious about, I’ll follow. That exploring is how you become who you are.

Trust the depth of your curiosity over your expertise

Imposter syndrome asks the wrong question. “Am I a good designer?” I can’t answer that, and I don’t need to. What I can do is trust the depth of my curiosity and my willingness to be good, and let those lead instead of my credentials.

That is how I get over the barrier between wanting to make something and actually making it, writing it or doing it.

There’s a companion idea I’m still working out: do one thing every day on blind faith, even when you suck at it, or can’t feel it, or can’t believe it. I read this as teaching yourself to trust yourself. I’m applying it to my own system. If I trust the process and follow through on these quarterly experiments, I think things will change.

Along the way, I remind myself that no time is lost. Even making a rambling recording about all this was useful: talking it through is how I digested what I’d written down.
